Each big cloud has a model service. You send requests, the cloud runs the model on its GPUs, and you never see a GPU. Each one offers the same four options. Only the names are different.

| What you want | AWS: Amazon Bedrock | Google: Agent Platform | Microsoft Foundry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared, pay per token | On-demand (Standard, Flex and Priority tiers) | Standard, Priority and Flex PayGo | Serverless: Global Standard, Data Zone Standard, Standard |
| Cheaper, not right away | Batch inference | Batch inference | Global Batch, Data Zone Batch |
| Reserved capacity | Provisioned Throughput, Reserved tier | Provisioned Throughput | Provisioned (PTU) |
| An open model on GPUs only you use | Custom Model Import, Bedrock Marketplace | Self-deployed open models (Model Garden) | Managed compute |

<Careful>

**Two services changed their names in 2026.** Google renamed Vertex AI to **Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform** (“Agent Platform” for short) on 22 April 2026. Microsoft renamed Azure AI Foundry to **Microsoft Foundry**. Many web pages, and even some API addresses, still use the old names.

</Careful>

## Shared, pay per token: the restaurant

This is how Botley works today. You pay a price per million tokens in, and another per million tokens out. You pay nothing when nobody uses it, and you don't wait for any machine to start.

Here are prices for two popular open-weight models on all three clouds, so you can compare like with like:

| Model | Amazon Bedrock | Google Agent Platform | Microsoft Foundry (Global) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Llama 3.3 70B | $0.72 in / $0.72 out | $0.72 in / $0.72 out | $0.71 in / $0.71 out |
| gpt-oss-120b | $0.15 in / $0.60 out | $0.09 in / $0.36 out | $0.15 in / $0.60 out |

<Note>

Prices in US dollars per million tokens, on-demand, US regions (Bedrock us-east-1, Foundry East US). Sources: AWS Price List for Bedrock, Google Cloud generative AI pricing, Azure retail prices. Checked 19 October 2026.

</Note>

The tiers trade price for speed. On Bedrock, **Flex** is 50% cheaper but slower, and **Priority** costs 75% more and is served first. Google's Agent Platform has Flex and Priority PayGo with the same idea, and Microsoft Foundry offers Flex and Priority processing on Global Standard.

Microsoft's names tell you **where your requests are processed**. **Global Standard** can use Microsoft's data centers anywhere. **Data Zone Standard** stays inside a zone: the US, the EU or Asia Pacific. **Standard** keeps it in the Azure geography where you created the resource. AWS does something similar with **cross-region inference**: a “geographic” profile keeps requests in the US, the EU or Asia Pacific, and a “global” one can use any region and is about 10% cheaper.

## Batch: cheaper, but not right away

You send a big file of requests and collect the answers later. All three clouds charge 50% less for batch on the models that support it. Microsoft's target is to finish within 24 hours. Batch is no good for a live chat with Botley, but very good for jobs like “summarize yesterday's 40,000 support tickets”.

## Reserved capacity: the private room

You pay by the hour for a fixed amount of model capacity, whether you use it or not. In return, your requests don't compete with other customers for that capacity.

- **Bedrock Provisioned Throughput:** billed per hour for each “model unit”. You can buy it with no commitment, or commit for 1 or 6 months. Some models you fine-tune on Bedrock can only be run this way. Bedrock also has a **Reserved tier**: you reserve tokens per minute for 1 or 3 months, and traffic above that is served at Standard prices.
- **Agent Platform Provisioned Throughput:** billed in units called GSUs, for a fixed term of 1 week (Google's own models only), 1 month, 3 months or 1 year. Traffic above what you bought is charged at normal pay-per-token prices.
- **Foundry Provisioned (PTU):** billed per hour for each Provisioned Throughput Unit, or at a lower price if you reserve PTUs for 1 month or 1 year.

<Note>

A “model unit”, a GSU and a PTU are measures of model capacity. They are not GPUs. Each provider has a calculator to work out how many you need for your traffic.

</Note>

## An open model on GPUs only you use

This is the middle path from Chapter 1. You pick an open-weight model, the provider runs it on GPUs that only you use, and you don't manage the servers.

- **Bedrock Custom Model Import:** you upload the weights of a supported model family (including Llama, Mistral, Mixtral, Qwen and gpt-oss) and call it like any Bedrock model. Importing is free. You pay per minute for each copy of the model that is running, counted in 5-minute blocks, plus a monthly storage fee.
- **Bedrock Marketplace:** over 100 more models, deployed to SageMaker AI endpoints and called through Bedrock.
- **Agent Platform self-deployed open models:** you choose a model from Model Garden and the machine and GPU to run it on. Google says these “aren't serverless”. You pay per node per hour, including when it is idle.
- **Foundry managed compute:** the model runs on virtual machines that only you use, billed per hour for the machines.

This gives you the model you choose and steady speed, without running Kubernetes. But like your own GPUs, you pay by the hour, even when nobody is using it.

## What they promise about your data

Sam's second question was about data. Here is what each provider says, in its own words:

| Provider | What it says |
|---|---|
| Amazon Bedrock | Your content “is not used to improve the base models and is not shared with any model providers.” |
| Google Agent Platform | Google “won't use your data to train or fine-tune any AI/ML models without your prior permission or instruction.” |
| Microsoft Foundry | Prompts and answers “are NOT available to OpenAI or other providers” and “are NOT used to train any generative AI foundation models without your permission.” |

<Careful>

**“Not used for training” is not the same as “never stored”.** Each provider can keep some data for a while, for example for abuse monitoring or caching. Google's Gemini caches data by default for up to 24 hours, and you can turn this off. Microsoft may keep flagged requests for human review unless you are approved to switch that off. Bedrock has a data-retention setting for each region. Read the data pages for the exact service and model you use, and set them before you go live.

</Careful>

## Models don't last forever

On a model service, the provider decides when a model version is switched off.

- **Bedrock:** a model goes from Active to Legacy to End-of-Life. Most get at least 6 months' notice, some only 45 days. After End-of-Life, requests fail.
- **Microsoft Foundry:** generally available models usually retire 18 months after launch (12 months for models from some partners such as Anthropic, DeepSeek and Mistral). You get at least 60 days' notice, and the dates can't be extended.

So on the managed path, plan to test and move to new model versions a few times a year.

<NoSillyQuestions>

<QA q={"Can I use Claude through the cloud services?"}>

Yes. Claude is available on Bedrock, on Google's Agent Platform and on Microsoft Foundry, as a managed model. You still can't download it.

</QA>

<QA q={"Does a managed service keep my data in my country?"}>

It can, if you choose the right options. To stay in one country, pick a region in that country, use a Standard deployment on Foundry (it stays in that Azure geography), and call Bedrock in that region without a cross-region profile. Data zones and geographic profiles cover groups of countries, such as the EU. Check the documents for the exact model, because not every model is offered in every region.

</QA>

<QA q={"Which managed option is cheapest?"}>

It depends on how busy you are. Pay per token is cheapest for low or uneven traffic. Reserved capacity and dedicated GPUs only win when you keep them busy most of the time. Chapter 9 shows how to work it out.

</QA>

<Takeaways>

- Bedrock, Agent Platform and Foundry each offer pay per token, batch, reserved capacity, and dedicated GPUs for open models.
- Vertex AI is now Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform. Azure AI Foundry is now Microsoft Foundry.
- Batch costs 50% less but is not for live chat.
- Reserved capacity and dedicated GPUs are billed by the hour, busy or not.
- All three promise not to train on your data without permission, but some data can still be stored for a while. Check the settings.
- Managed models are retired on the provider's schedule. Plan for regular upgrades.

</Takeaways>
